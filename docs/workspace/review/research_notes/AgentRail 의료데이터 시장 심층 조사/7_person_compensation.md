## 1. Participant compensation in large research programs (All of Us, UK Biobank, Our Future Health, MVP, IRB payment norms)

### Takeaway
Large programs pay people a token amount or nothing at all: All of Us pays $25 per visit, Our Future Health gives a voucher, and UK Biobank and the Million Veteran Program are altruistic. Pay norms are shaped by "undue influence" rules, not by what the data is worth. The value goes to researchers through access fees and science, while participants get small one-off payments, results returned to them, and a public-good story.

### Cited Findings
- **All of Us (NIH):** participants receive **$25** for their time, generally after an in-person visit where samples or measurements are taken. Enrollment takes about 1.5–2 h, mostly online. Open to US residents aged 18 and over. [P, enrolling-site pages, undated] — [Yale Medicine trial listing](https://yalemedicine.org/clinical-trials/all-of-us); [Boston Medical Center](https://bmc.org/all-us-research-program); [UMiami Health News](https://news.umiamihealth.org/en/you-can-help-to-advance-genetic-research/) [S]
- All of Us released data on **>747,000 participants**, including **535,000 genomes** linked to medical records, on **June 30, 2026**. NIH called it the world's largest integrated health database. [S, 2026] — [The Next Web, Jul 1 2026](https://thenextweb.com/news/nih-all-of-us-largest-genomics-health-database); [Boston Globe, Jun 30 2026](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2026/06/30/nation/nih-announces-worlds-largest-integrated-health-database/)
- Older All of Us figures: about **873,000 consented** and **414,000 with WGS** (earlier GenomeWeb planning coverage, 2024–25). [S] — [GenomeWeb](https://genomeweb.com/sequencing/all-us-research-program-unfazed-funding-cuts-lays-out-plans-through-2026)
- **All of Us budget cut:** down about **72% since 2023**. GenomeWeb gives the drop as 71%, from more than $500M (2023) to about $150M (2025). FY2026 funding is about $153M. CEO Josh Denny said "the pace of enrollment has been curtailed significantly." Adult enrollment sites were sharply reduced and adult biosample collection is paused. More than 50 medical organizations wrote to Congress warning that what had been built could be lost. [S, 2025–2026] — [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/nih-all-of-us-largest-genomics-health-database); [GenomeWeb](https://genomeweb.com/sequencing/all-us-research-program-unfazed-funding-cuts-lays-out-plans-through-2026); [CASRAI](https://casrai.org/news/nih-all-of-us-pediatric-enrollment-funding-2026)
- **Our Future Health (UK):** a voucher scheme started for new participants in **December 2023**. The voucher is offered after the participant completes the questionnaire and gives a blood sample, and it can be donated to the OFH charity. OFH said it introduced the scheme for equality and diversity in research. [P, 2023] — [OFH news: voucher scheme](https://ourfuturehealth.org.uk/news/introducing-our-voucher-scheme-and-what-it-means-for-equality-in-health-research/)
  - The voucher amount is commonly reported as £10, but the search summaries did not confirm it, so I list it as a gap.
- **OFH scale:** more than **2.5 million volunteers** joined (Feb 2026) [S, 2026]; more than 1 million "full participants" (Feb 2025) [S]; target is up to **5 million**. — [Loughborough Univ., Feb 2026](https://lboro.ac.uk/internal/news/2026/february/join-our-future-health-today/); [OFH Taking part](https://ourfuturehealth.org.uk/join) [P]
- **UK Biobank:** recruited **500,000** people aged 40–69 in **2006–2010**. Researchers pay for access: about **£9,000 for 3 years** for the full dataset including WGS, and **£500 + VAT** for low- and middle-income-country researchers. A donor-funded access fund (backed by AstraZeneca, J&J and others) covers reduced fees. More than **22,000 researchers in 60+ countries** use the data. Value comes in through access fees, not participant pay. [P/S] — [UK Biobank researchers page](https://www.ukbiobank.ac.uk/researchers/) [P]; [GenomeWeb WGS release](https://www.genomeweb.com/sequencing/uk-biobank-releases-whole-genome-sequencing-data-all-500k-participants) [S]; [Research Professional News](https://www.researchprofessionalnews.com/?p=527033) [S]
- **Million Veteran Program (VA):** the 1,000,000th veteran enrolled on **Nov 8, 2023** [P, VA]. Enrollment was **1,091,487** as of Nov 20, 2025 [S] and **more than 1.1 million** as of Sept 2026 [S]. MVP describes itself as the largest and most diverse genetic research program within a health system. — [VA Columbia release](https://www.va.gov/columbia-south-carolina-health-care/news-releases/vas-million-veteran-program-makes-history-1-millionth-veteran-joins-mvp/); [Military.com, Nov 2025](https://365.military.com/feature/2025/11/21/inside-million-veteran-programs-growth-and-what-it-means-va-healthcare.html); [American Legion, Sept 16 2026](https://www.legion.org/information-center/news/veterans-healthcare/2026/september/more-than-1-million-veterans-enrolled-in-million-veterans-program)
- **FDA payment guidance (information sheet, updated Jan 2018, non-binding):**
  - Payment is not considered a benefit, and IRBs must judge whether an amount could be coercive or create undue influence.
  - Payment should be "just and fair."
  - Reimbursing travel, parking and lodging is not considered undue influence.
  - Payment should accrue as the study progresses and must not depend on completing the study. A small completion bonus is allowed.
  - The amount and schedule must appear in the consent form.
  - [P, 2018] — [FDA guidance](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/payment-and-reimbursement-research-subjects); [Outsourcing-Pharma](https://outsourcing-pharma.com/Article/2018/02/01/Patient-payment-in-clinical-trials-US-FDA-flags-undue-influence-reimbursement-and-consent) [S]
- Institutional IRB policies follow the same framing, e.g. [Duke IRB 2021](https://irb.duhs.duke.edu/sites/default/files/2022-04/compensation_payments_to_research_participants_3-18-2021.pdf) and [Northwestern IRB](https://irb.northwestern.edu/docs/research-participant-payments-general-1902.pdf). [P]

### Inferences
- IRB norms treat payment as payment for time and inconvenience, never for the value of the data. This means a "data dividend" tied to downstream commercial value has no clear home in current US research-ethics guidance. It would need new framing, for example data as property or revenue sharing outside the consent payment.
- The All of Us budget collapse (−72%) shows the fragility of a publicly funded, low-pay model. It also creates an opening for alternative funding models such as industry access fees (UK Biobank style) or participant-aligned marketplaces.

### Gaps
- Exact OFH voucher value (believed to be £10) and how many vouchers have been redeemed: not confirmed.
- Whether All of Us returns genetic results (HCP/PGx reports) at scale, and how many: not retrieved in this pass.
- MVP payment policy: likely none, but I found no source confirming that explicitly.
- UK Biobank participant payment: I found no source stating it explicitly, though participants are widely described as volunteers.

## 2. 23andMe: bankruptcy, sale, deletions, GSK deal, consent lessons

### Takeaway
23andMe is the reference case of a consumer DNA asset being monetized through pharma deals: a $300M GSK equity deal in 2018, then a sale in bankruptcy for $305M in 2025. Customers never shared in that value. The bankruptcy prompted about 1.9M–2M deletion requests (about 15% of customers), and state attorneys general intervened over consent transferring to a new owner.

### Cited Findings
- 23andMe filed for Chapter 11 in **March 2025**. [S] — [HIPAA Journal](https://www.hipaajournal.com/genetic-testing-company-23andme-files-for-bankruptcy/)
- **Regeneron** initially won the auction at **$256M**. One source says $265M, which looks like a typo. Regeneron did not close. [S, 2025] — [AOL/Fortune](https://www.aol.com/6b-valuation-bankruptcy-rescue-wojcicki-183223119.html); [Yahoo News](https://www.yahoo.com/news/23andme-officially-sold-happen-customers-194104258.html)
- **TTAM Research Institute** (a nonprofit led by Anne Wojcicki) bought substantially all assets for **$305M**: the Personal Genome Service, Research Services and Lemonaid Health. The court approved the sale in late June 2025, and it closed **July 2025** (sources say July 12 or July 14). [S/P] — [MedTech Dive](https://www.medtechdive.com/news/judge-approves-23andme-sale-wojcicki-TTAM/752225/); [Paul, Weiss](https://paulweiss.com/insights/client-news/23andme-wins-eighth-circuit-decision-allowing-sale-to-move-forward) [P, counsel]
  - The Eighth Circuit denied California's emergency stay. [P, counsel] — [Paul, Weiss](https://paulweiss.com/insights/client-news/23andme-wins-eighth-circuit-decision-allowing-sale-to-move-forward)
- **Valuation collapse:** from a roughly **$6B** peak valuation to a $305M sale. [S] — [AOL](https://www.aol.com/6b-valuation-bankruptcy-rescue-wojcicki-183223119.html)
- **Deletion requests:** about **1.9 million people (about 15% of customers)** asked for deletion after the March 2025 filing [P, PA AG]. Other reports say about 2M of 15M. [S] — [PA Attorney General](https://www.attorneygeneral.gov/taking-action/ag-sunday-supports-proposed-sale-of-23andme-that-would-protect-consumers-dna-and-data/); [Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/news/23andme-officially-sold-happen-customers-194104258.html)
- **Customer base:** reported as about 13M (judge's memorandum) or 15M (press). These conflict. [S] — [Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/news/23andme-officially-sold-happen-customers-194104258.html)
- **Sale conditions (backed by several attorneys general):**
  - TTAM keeps allowing permanent deletion at any time, with deletions verifiable by state regulators.
  - Customers are contacted to choose about research use.
  - Part of the proceeds pays claims for the **~7 million** people hit by the 2023 breach.
  - **28 state AGs** led by NY AG Letitia James sued over privacy.
  - [P] — [PA Attorney General](https://www.attorneygeneral.gov/taking-action/ag-sunday-supports-proposed-sale-of-23andme-that-would-protect-consumers-dna-and-data/); [Alberta OIPC](https://oipc.ab.ca/update-from-the-information-and-privacy-commissioner-regarding-the-sale-of-23andme-including-its-genome-assets/)
- **GSK deal (July 2018):** a **$300M equity investment** and a 4-year drug-discovery collaboration with 50/50 cost and profit sharing, later extended to a 5th year. It ended in 2023, followed by 23andMe layoffs. [S, 2018/2023] — [MedCity News 2018](https://medcitynews.com/2018/07/gsk-23andme-sign-four-year-drug-discovery-partnership/); [Engadget 2018](https://www.engadget.com/2018-07-26-gsk-23andme-dna-database-drug-development.html); [DNA Explained 2023](https://dna-explained.com/2023/08/17/23andme-and-glaksosmithkline-partnership-ends-sparking-additional-layoffs/)
- **Consent:** 23andMe said research participation was opt-in, with "about 80%" of customers opting in. That figure appears only in a secondary blog and is unverified. Critics argued that consumers had understood consent to cover non-profit research, not pharma commercialization. [S] — [DNA Explained](https://dna-explained.com/2023/08/17/23andme-and-glaksosmithkline-partnership-ends-sparking-additional-layoffs/); [Tom's Guide 2018](https://www.tomsguide.com/uk/us/23andme-gsk-dna-data-deal,news-27685.html)

### Inferences
- **Lesson 1:** one-time broad consent given to a company does not hold up against changes of control. Bankruptcy turned consent into a transferable asset, and regulators had to bolt on deletion and verification rights after the fact.
- **Lesson 2:** about 15% of customers deleted their data in weeks, a measurable "exit" response when trust breaks. A person-centric model with revocable, per-use consent would make that exit routine rather than panic-driven.
- **Lesson 3:** the value was realized at roughly $300M from GSK in 2018 and $305M at sale in 2025. Spread across about 13–15M customers, that is about $20–23 per customer per event, which gives a rough benchmark for per-person data value. This is my own arithmetic, not a sourced figure.

### Gaps
- TTAM's post-close research-consent re-confirmation rates: not found.
- Status of the California and other state suits after closing: not found.

## 3. Person-centric data ventures: what happened and why

### Takeaway
Most "get paid for your data" ventures from 2014 to 2021 failed or pivoted:
- **LunaDNA:** closed Jan 2024 for lack of funds.
- **Nebula:** pivoted to selling consumer WGS, was acquired by ProPhase for $14.6M, and its business was later put up for sale.
- **Datacoup** (about $10/month max) and **Hu-manity.co:** went quiet.

Cooperatives such as MIDATA survive by rejecting individual payouts and reinvesting surplus collectively. The common failure modes are thin buyer demand, tiny per-person payouts, high cost of acquiring data contributors, and dependence on venture or crowdfunded capital.

### Cited Findings
- **LunaDNA / LunaPBC:**
  - Closed **Jan 31, 2024**. Its email to members said "Luna is Closing," citing a critical shortage of funds. [S, 2024] — [Inside Precision Medicine](https://www.insideprecisionmedicine.com/topics/precision-medicine/total-eclipse-of-lunadna-once-touted-genome-data-sharing-platform-goes-dark); [Global Genes RARE Daily](https://globalgenes.org/raredaily/lunadna-shutting-down)
  - Its SEC Reg A semiannual filing showed expenses exceeding revenue and **no cash or liquid assets as of June 30, 2022**, with "substantial doubt" about continuing as a going concern. [S, citing SEC filing] — same sources
  - What happens to the contributed data was unclear at closing. [S] — same sources
  - LunaDNA's model was to give members shares in exchange for data. That detail comes from my background knowledge, not from these search results, so verify it.
- **Nebula Genomics** (co-founded by George Church):
  - Moved from a blockchain data-sharing concept to selling consumer whole-genome sequencing for ancestry and lifestyle reports.
  - ProPhase Labs acquired it in 2021 for **$14.6M** in stock and cash. [S] — [GenomeWeb](https://www.genomeweb.com/node/618516)
  - Late 2024: ProPhase reportedly closed its lab, moved WGS into a subsidiary called "DNA Complete," and sought to sell the genomics business (Feb 2025 press release). This is from a non-authoritative user wiki. [S, low quality] — [h600.org wiki](https://h600.org/wiki/Nebula)
- **Hu-manity.co (#My31 app, 2018):**
  - Framed personal data ownership as the "31st human right" and recorded consent as property on IBM's blockchain. It stored no medical data itself.
  - Planned a marketplace where users could be paid, and later partnered with Orb Health.
  - Coverage noted that US law generally gives data ownership to the collecting entity.
  - I found no evidence of current operations or payouts. [S, 2018] — [TechCrunch 2018](https://techcrunch.com/2018/07/18/hu-manity-wants-to-create-a-health-data-marketplace-with-help-from-blockchain/); [FierceBiotech](https://www.fiercebiotech.com/medtech/hu-manity-co-launches-blockchain-app-to-help-users-sell-their-healthcare-data); [Reuters Events](https://www.reutersevents.com/pharma/node/62381)
- **Datacoup (2014):** paid up to **$10/month** for access to social accounts and card transactions. Actual payouts depended on which data streams a user shared. I found no confirmation of when it shut down. [S, 2014] — [MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2014/09/08/171469/datacoup-wants-to-buy-your-credit-card-and-facebook-data/)
- **CitizenMe (UK):** an app for aggregating personal data and sharing it anonymously with companies in exchange for cash, discounts or donations, with PayPal payouts for surveys. It appears to still operate, but this comes from an AI-generated profile and is unverified. [S] — [Dealroom](https://app.dealroom.co/companies/citizenme)
- **MIDATA (Switzerland, founded 2015):**
  - A nonprofit cooperative acting as a data trustee, with one-member-one-vote governance.
  - Companies pay fees to use data for research. Surplus is reinvested by the General Assembly in public-benefit research.
  - **No individual financial incentives:** co-founder Ernst Hafen explicitly rejects paying people for their data.
  - The platform is open source, and MIDATA supports regional or national sister cooperatives.
  - [P/S] — [MIDATA cooperative page](https://www.midata.coop/en/cooperative/) [P]; [Nesta](https://www.nesta.org.uk/me-my-data-and-I/midatacoop) [S]; [ETH research collection](https://www.research-collection.ethz.ch/entities/publication/18c88665-1971-4147-b8bf-4b47d7cf55f5) [P, academic]
- **Salus Coop (Barcelona):** aims for citizen-driven collaborative governance of health data. Few details found. [S] — [P2P Foundation wiki](https://wiki.p2pfoundation.net/Unleashing_the_Potential_of_Ethical_and_Cooperative_Health_and_Care_Data)
- **Driver's Seat Cooperative** (gig-worker data, not health; founded 2019):
  - Planned to sell pooled driver data to cities and transport agencies and share profits with members. Its app showed drivers their real hourly wage after expenses.
  - Reported about 600 workers in 40 cities (undated).
  - I found no evidence that a data sale closed. I recall it wound down around 2023–24, but that is unverified. [S] — [Platform Cooperativism library](https://resources.platform.coop/resources/co-op-helps-uber-lyft-drivers-use-data-to-maximize-earnings); [Mozilla Foundation](https://www.mozillafoundation.org/es/blog/gig-workers-find-solidarity-through-data/); [Axios](https://www.axios.com/reform-gig-work-co-ops-ride-hailing-uber-lyft-ee4848cf-d80c-4fa7-9297-10a19b23196d.html)
- **Tumor-sequencing payment pilot (Merck-linked survey):** 63 of 64 patients were willing to sell access to their tumor-sequencing data, provided they kept ownership and the data went only to the paying party. [S] — [IAPP](https://iapp.org/news/a/merck-finds-patients-willing-to-sell-access-to-data/)

### Inferences
- **Demand-side problem:** buyers such as pharma and payers want large, linked, longitudinal, curated cohorts like UK Biobank or All of Us, or EHR aggregators. Fragmented individual uploads do not give them that, so marketplaces built on individual uploads struggled to find paying buyers.
- **Unit-economics problem:** individual payouts are small (Datacoup at most about $10/month; 23andMe-implied value of about $20/person per deal), so they do not motivate sustained participation. They also run into IRB "undue influence" norms when used in research.
- **What survives:** governance-centric models (MIDATA-style cooperatives and trusts, Light Collective-style civic trusts) that offer control, transparency and collective benefit seem more durable than per-record cash. This matches the survey evidence that control features such as deletion and permission before reuse raise willingness.

### Gaps
- **Genomes.io, Ocean Protocol health pilots, HealthBank (Swiss cooperative), Embleema, Hippocratic AI:** I found no reliable results in this pass, so their outcomes remain unknown.
- **MIDATA:** current member counts and revenue not found.
- **Datacoup:** exact shutdown date not found.

## 4. Survey evidence on willingness to share and compensation (2016–2026)

### Takeaway
Willingness to share is high in principle: a pooled 77% across 65 studies. It depends heavily on who receives the data (clinicians 70–86% vs tech companies 7–15%) and on control features. Payment changes who participates more than how many: in Briscoe 2020, 11.7% would donate free, 50.6% would share only if paid, and 37.8% would not share at all.

### Cited Findings
- **Briscoe et al., PLoS ONE 2020, 15(3):e0229044** (about 2,020 US-representative adults):
  - **11.7%** would donate genetic data altruistically, **50.6%** would share only if paid, and **37.8%** would not share at any price.
  - Median net expected payment was **−$75** for altruists and **+$95** for those wanting payment.
  - The features that most increased willingness were the right to deletion, no sale or sharing beyond the collector, and specific permission before reuse.
  - Willingness was lower when data could go to pharma or the federal government.
  - [P, 2020; seen via Penn State release, secondary] — [Penn State News](https://www.psu.edu/news/research/story/more-half-americans-want-money-control-exchange-genetic-data); [PSU Pure record](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/evolving-public-views-on-the-value-of-ones-dna-and-expectations-f/)
- **Systematic review (65 studies, data 2015–2022, mostly high-income countries):** willingness to share health data ranged from **24% to 100%**, with a **pooled 77%**. [P, academic] — [JCU repository PDF](https://researchonline.jcu.edu.au/88898/1/88898.pdf)
- **Rock Health Digital Health Consumer Adoption surveys:**
  - **2016:** 77% wanted to share health information. **62%** would share freely for research, vs **42%** for money. [S] — [MobiHealthNews](https://www.mobihealthnews.com/news/report-consumer-adoption-digital-health-reaches-tipping-point-2016)
  - **2017** (about 4,000 adults): willingness was **86%** for physicians and **10%** for tech companies. Confidence in tech companies' security was 24%. [S] — [Becker's](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/healthcare-information-technology/digital-health/who-do-patients-want-to-share-health-data-with/)
  - **2018** (fall 2018, about 4,000 adults): **11%** willing to share with tech companies, **72%** with their doctor, **35%** with research institutions. Among the 11%, Google was the most trusted (60%). [S, 2019] — [CNBC Feb 2019](https://www.cnbc.com/2019/02/13/consumers-dont-trust-tech-companies-with-health-data-rock-health.html)
  - **2022** (more than 8,000 adults): **70%** willing to share with clinicians, **15%** with health tech companies, **7%** with non-health tech companies. [S] — [MobiHealthNews](https://www.mobihealthnews.com/news/more-consumers-are-using-digital-health-manage-complex-health-conditions-rock-health-says); [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/consumers-high-use-of-digital-health-tools-doesn-t-match-their-data-privacy-concerns)
- **JAMA Netw Open 2019** (1,246-patient survey on EHR data and biospecimens): most patients were willing to share for research. I could not verify the "~67%" figure (the fetch failed). [S] — [CancerNetwork summary](https://cancernetwork.com/view/are-most-patients-willing-share-medical-data)
- **JAMA Netw Open, Jan 2022 (Penn conjoint study):** willingness was highest when university hospitals used digital health data for research with all four privacy protections, and lowest when tech companies used it for marketing without protections. [S] — [MobiHealthNews](https://www.mobihealthnews.com/news/survey-privacy-protections-boost-consumers-willingness-share-health-data); [Chief Healthcare Executive](https://chiefhealthcareexecutive.com/view/many-don-t-want-data-shared-even-for-health-studies)
- **"82% think patients should be paid":** reported by HealthExec as a survey finding that about 82% of Americans think patients should be compensated for sharing health data. The date, sponsor and sample were not retrievable (the fetch failed with DNS). [S, undated, likely 2023–24] — [HealthExec](https://healthexec.com/topics/patient-care/digital-transformation/americans-open-sharing-data-drug-development-ai)
- **HIMSS/Statista 2021:** most US patients were at least somewhat willing to share data for benefits such as earlier detection of illness. [S] — [Statista](https://www.statista.com/statistics/1249872/willingness-of-patients-to-share-health-data-in-the-us)

### Inferences
- Payment roughly quadruples the share of willing participants in the genetic-data case (11.7% → 62.3% willing in some form). About 38% are unreachable at any price, which points to an irreducible trust or privacy floor.
- Compensation and control interact. Rock Health 2016 found people more willing to share free for research (62%) than for money (42%), while Briscoe 2020 found the opposite for genetic data. Framing (research vs commerce) and recipient (pharma, government, tech) matter as much as price. This conflict is worth flagging in the final report.
- Trust in tech companies is persistently low (7–15%). Any marketplace run by a tech intermediary starts at a trust disadvantage unless it borrows institutional trust (hospital, university, cooperative) or offers verifiable control.

### Gaps
- No 2024–2026 Rock Health figure on sharing with tech companies was found.
- Pew 2023–2026 figures on health-data sharing were not found in this pass.
- The original source, date and methodology for the 82% "should be paid" figure are unverified.
- The exact headline percentages in JAMA Netw Open 2019 are unverified.

## 5. Dynamic consent evidence and patient advocacy / data-dividend policy

### Takeaway
Evidence that dynamic consent improves trust or retention is mostly qualitative and perception-based. The 2025 rapid review found 12 papers and no quantified retention effect, and a well-trusted cohort (EXCEED) did not want it. Advocacy has shifted toward collective, patient-led governance (Light Collective). Newsom's 2019 "data dividend" never moved beyond a concept.

### Cited Findings
- **Lay et al., rapid review, Research Ethics Review (Jan 2025):** screened **1,611** papers and included **12**. It identified **31 benefits and 8 challenges**. Retention benefits were attributed to autonomy, trust and communication. Most evidence was qualitative, and quantitative effects on recruitment and retention remain unmeasured. [P] — [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/3071e42f3eb34dde848490eab44a267d)
- **Wallace & Miola, BMC Medical Ethics 2021** (EXCEED cohort focus groups): participants had very high trust in the study, there was **no strong support** for adding a dynamic-consent interface, and the authors warned that change could disturb that trust. The sample was small. [P] — [BMC Med Ethics PDF](https://bmcmedethics.biomedcentral.com/counter/pdf/10.1186/s12910-021-00583-w.pdf)
- **Budin-Ljøsne et al., BMC Medical Ethics 2017 (data 2016) workshop study:** stakeholders saw dynamic consent as a possible tool for retention and consent management. This was opinion, not outcome data. [P] — [BMC Med Ethics](https://bmcmedethics.biomedcentral.com/track/pdf/10.1186/s12910-016-0162-9); [Oxford ORA](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:a671615d-ebed-4a7e-987a-481167b1056f/files/dh702q697z)
- **The Light Collective (patient advocacy):**
  - Launched a Patient AI Rights Initiative (2023) and published 7 "Collective AI Rights for Patients," with patient-led governance as the first right. It calls for an independent patient-led AI governance body.
  - Is piloting a **civic data trust** for genetic-mutation patient communities on social media.
  - Partners with the National Academy of Medicine's AI Code of Conduct and has representation in CancerX and the Health AI Partnership.
  - [P/S, 2024–2025] — [TechTarget](https://www.techtarget.com/patientengagement/news/366584189/Patient-advocates-explore-a-patient-bill-of-rights-for-AI); [J Participatory Med 2025](https://jopm.jmir.org/2025/1/e69942); [MIT Solve: Light Collective Data Trust](https://solve.mit.edu/solutions/50745)

### Inferences
- A product claim that "per-use consent improves trust or retention" is plausible but not yet backed by quantitative evidence. Any rigorous claim should be framed as a hypothesis, or tested with an A/B retention metric.
- Well-trusted institutional cohorts may not need dynamic consent. The value of dynamic consent is likely highest where trust is low or contested: commercial, cross-border and post-acquisition settings such as the 23andMe case.
- Policy momentum is toward governance and control (deletion rights, state genetic-privacy laws, AG enforcement) rather than mandated cash dividends.

### Gaps
- No RCT or quantitative cohort study measuring dynamic consent's effect on retention was found.
- Whether any US state enacted a data-dividend law: none found. Federal proposals (e.g. Sen. Kennedy's "Own Your Own Data Act," 2019) were not checked.
- Light Collective data-trust pilot outcomes and metrics: not found.
