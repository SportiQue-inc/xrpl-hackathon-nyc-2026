## 2. US FTC enforcement (location and health data)

### Takeaway
From 2023 to 2026 the FTC built up a set of orders that ban or limit the sale of sensitive location and health data without affirmative express consent: X-Mode, InMarket, Gravy/Venntel, Mobilewalla and Kochava for location; GoodRx, BetterHelp and Cerebral for health. It also expanded the Health Breach Notification Rule (HBNR) in 2024. Money penalties are modest (Kochava had none). The real constraints are the conduct terms: consent verification, supplier assessment programs and data deletion.

### Cited Findings
- **Kochava (2026):** The FTC announced a proposed settlement on May 4, 2026 with Kochava and its subsidiary Collective Data Solutions, in a case that began in August 2022. The order was filed in D. Idaho and needs court approval. It bars selling or disclosing sensitive location data without affirmative express consent, and then only for a service the consumer directly requested. Sensitive locations include medical facilities, religious organizations, shelters, and military sites. It requires a supplier assessment program to verify consumer consent, incident reports, and a way to withdraw consent. **There is no monetary penalty.** Kochava had already agreed to similar limits in a class-action settlement [S, 2026] — [White & Case](https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/ftc-settles-data-broker-kochava-over-sale-sensitive-location-data-key-takeaways); [The Record](https://therecord.media/ftc-bans-kochava-location-data-sales); [Security Boulevard, May 2026](https://securityboulevard.com/2026/05/ftc-to-ban-data-broker-kochava-from-selling-americans-location-data/). Bloomberg Law first reported a settlement notice on Feb 27, 2026 [S] — [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bgov.com/ip-law/kochava-inc-settles-location-data-trade-practice-suit-with-ftc). Whether the court has approved the order as of Oct 2026 was not confirmed.
- **Gravy Analytics / Venntel (final Jan 14, 2025):** The FTC alleged the companies used geofencing to identify and sell lists of people who visited healthcare facilities and places of worship. The final order bans selling, disclosing or using sensitive location data, except for national security or law enforcement, and requires a sensitive location data program. The vote was 5-0 [S, 2025] — [Hunton](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/ftc-finalizes-orders-against-data-brokers-over-sensitive-location-data)
- **Mobilewalla (final Jan 2025):** The FTC alleged it tracked and sold visits to healthcare facilities and places of worship. The order also limits its use of real-time-bidding data, the first time the FTC alleged that RTB data harvesting was unfair [S, 2025] — [Hunton](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/ftc-finalizes-orders-against-data-brokers-over-sensitive-location-data); [Digital Policy Alert](https://digitalpolicyalert.org/event/26019-issued-ftc-order-against-mobilewalla-over-collection-and-sale-of-sensitive-location-data-without-verification-of-consumers-consent)
- **X-Mode Social / Outlogic (proposed Jan 9, 2024; final Apr 12, 2024):** This was the FTC's first ban on selling sensitive location data, such as visits to clinics and places of worship. The company must delete previously collected data and products built from it unless consumers consent or the data is deidentified [P (search summary), 2024] — [FTC, Jan 2024](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/01/ftc-order-prohibits-data-broker-x-mode-social-outlogic-selling-sensitive-location-data); [FTC final, Apr 2024](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/04/ftc-finalizes-order-x-mode-successor-outlogic-prohibiting-it-sharing-or-selling-sensitive-location)
- **InMarket (proposed Jan 18, 2024; final May 1, 2024):** The order bans selling or licensing any precise location data and any product that groups consumers by sensitive location [P (search summary), 2024] — [FTC, Jan 2024](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/01/ftc-order-will-ban-inmarket-selling-precise-consumer-location-data); [FTC final, May 2024](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/05/ftc-finalizes-order-inmarket-prohibiting-it-selling-or-sharing-precise-location-data). The FTC noted that none of these datasets were alleged to include names or SSNs, and the location data was still treated as sensitive [P] — [FTC Tech blog, Mar 2024](https://www.ftc.gov/policy/advocacy-research/tech-at-ftc/2024/03/ftc-cracks-down-mass-data-collectors-closer-look-avast-x-mode-inmarket)
- **GoodRx (Feb 2023):** This was the first enforcement action under the HBNR. It carried a **$1.5M civil penalty** and the first ban on sharing health data for targeted advertising [S, 2023] — [Davis Polk](https://www.davispolk.com/insights/client-update/ftc-brings-first-enforcement-action-under-health-breach-notification-rule)
- **BetterHelp (final July 2023):** The order bans sharing sensitive mental-health data for advertising, for example with Facebook and Criteo, and requires a **$7.8M** payment. Refund notices went to about 800,000 consumers in May 2024 [P (search summary), 2023/2024] — [FTC, Jul 2023](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2023/07/ftc-gives-final-approval-order-banning-betterhelp-sharing-sensitive-health-data-advertising); [FTC, May 2024](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/05/betterhelp-customers-will-begin-receiving-notices-about-refunds-related-2023-privacy-settlement-ftc)
- **Cerebral (2024 order):** The case covered sharing health data for advertising and deceptive cancellation practices. In May 2025, **more than $5M** in refunds went to 40,249 consumers [P (search summary), 2025] — [FTC, May 2025](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/05/more-5-million-refunds-sent-consumers-result-ftcs-action-against-cerebral-over-deceptive). The total 2024 order amount (often reported as about $7M) was not confirmed in this session.
- **Flo Health:** The FTC's 2021 settlement with Flo had no monetary penalty (background knowledge, not verified this session). In private litigation, a San Francisco jury on **Aug 1, 2025** unanimously found **Meta liable under the California Invasion of Privacy Act (CIPA)** for collecting Flo users' reproductive health data through an SDK. CIPA allows up to $5,000 per violation, and damages are to be set by Judge Donato (N.D. Cal. 21-cv-00757). Flo, Google and Flurry settled before the verdict [S, 2025] — [Lawdragon](https://www.lawdragon.com/news-features/2025-08-25-big-tech-on-trial-jury-finds-meta-liable-for-misusing-women-health-data); [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/litigation/metas-health-privacy-trial-loss-spotlights-power-of-wiretapping)
- **HBNR update (finalized April 2024):** The amendments clarify that the rule covers health apps and similar technologies not covered by HIPAA, and that a "breach" includes unauthorized disclosures, not just hacks [P (search summary), 2024] — [FTC HBNR final rule PDF](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/hbnr_final_rule_04_25.pdf)
- **PADFAA (Protecting Americans' Data from Foreign Adversaries Act):** In effect since June 2024 and enforced by the FTC. It bars data brokers from providing sensitive data to foreign adversaries (China, Russia, Iran, North Korea). On **Feb 9, 2026** the FTC sent **warning letters to 13 data brokers** about selling data that reveals military status. No completed enforcement action was found as of Oct 2026 [P/S, 2026] — [Orrick InfoBytes, Feb 2026](https://infobytes.orrick.com/2026-02-12/ftc-warns-data-brokers-of-possible-violations-of-foreign-adversary-data-protection-law/); [FTC warning letter example](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/lexisnexis-risk-solutions-inc-warning-letter.pdf)

### Inferences
- The FTC's standard order now requires affirmative express consent, verification that suppliers obtained consent, and deletion of tainted datasets. In effect, buyers are being asked to prove the provenance of their data, which is exactly what a consent-based, auditable data channel offers.
- Monetary penalties are small, from $0 for Kochava to $7.8M for BetterHelp. Private litigation, such as the Flo/Meta CIPA verdict with potential per-violation damages, may now be the bigger financial risk.

### Gaps
- No annual count of FTC privacy actions for 2025–2026 was found. No 2025–2026 FTC health-data action under the current Commission was found beyond the Kochava settlement and the PADFAA letters.

## 3. US state laws, CFPB, and DOJ

### Takeaway
As federal data-broker rulemaking stalled (CFPB withdrew its rule in May 2025), states and national-security rules filled the gap. These include Washington's My Health My Data Act (MHMDA) with a private right of action, Nevada and Connecticut consumer health data laws, California's DROP mass-deletion platform (live Jan 2026, brokers required to process requests from Aug 2026), Texas AG enforcement under its data broker law, and the DOJ bulk sensitive data rule restricting genomic and health data flows to countries of concern.

### Cited Findings
- **Washington MHMDA:** The first class action was *Maxwell v. Amazon* (W.D. Wash. 2:25-cv-261, filed Feb 10, 2025). It alleges Amazon's advertising SDK collected consumer health data from third-party apps without consent and monetized it. There are seven claims, including the MHMDA, the Wiretap Act, the Stored Communications Act and the CFAA [S, 2025] — [WilmerHale](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/blogs/wilmerhale-privacy-and-cybersecurity-law/20250220-first-lawsuit-filed-under-washingtons-my-health-my-data-act); [Paul Hastings](https://paulhastings.com/insights/ph-privacy/biometrics-litigation-update-first-class-action-complaint-filed-under-washingtons-my-health-my-data-act-is-your-company-ready). The case status as of 2026 was not found.
- **Nevada SB 370:** Signed June 16, 2023 and effective March 31, 2024. It covers consumer health data, including from gyms and fitness or wellness services, and has **no private right of action** [S, 2023] — [WilmerHale](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/blogs/wilmerhale-privacy-and-cybersecurity-law/20230614-nevada-legislature-passes-consumer-health-data-privacy-bill)
- **Connecticut SB 3 (2023):** Amends the Connecticut Data Privacy Act to require opt-in consent for any processing or sale of consumer health data. It includes geofencing restrictions and has no private right of action [S, 2023] — [Manatt](https://www.manatt.com/insights/newsletters/privacy-and-data-security/connecticuts-new-law-on-consumer-health-data-is-n); [Hunton](https://hunton.com/privacy-and-information-security-law/connecticut-and-nevada-legislatures-pass-health-data-laws)
- **California Delete Act / DROP:** DROP (Delete Request and Opt-out Platform) opened to residents on **Jan 1, 2026**. One verified request goes to every registered broker. From **Aug 1, 2026**, brokers must check DROP every 45 days and process requests within 90 days. SB 361 doubled the fine for failing to register from $100 to **$200 per consumer per day**. The registry has more than 500–600 brokers; sources differ [S, 2026] — [Malwarebytes, Aug 2026](https://www.malwarebytes.com/blog/news/2026/08/californians-can-tell-data-brokers-to-drop-their-information); [TrustArc](https://trustarc.com/resource/california-delete-act-drop-platform-data-brokers/); [TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/news-california-drop-tool-deletes-data/)
- **Texas:** On Jan 13, 2025 the Texas AG sued Allstate and its subsidiary Arity. It was the first state AG lawsuit under a comprehensive privacy law (the TDPSA), and it also cites the Texas Data Broker Law because Arity had not registered. Arity paid app developers (GasBuddy, Fuel Rewards, Routely) to embed its SDK. Remedies sought include up to $7,500 per violation under the TDPSA and up to $10,000 per violation plus daily fines under the Data Broker Law [P/S, 2025] — [Texas OAG petition](https://oag.state.tx.us/sites/default/files/images/press/Allstate%20and%20Arity%20Petition%20Filed.pdf); [Hunton](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/texas-ag-sues-allstate-for-violations-of-texas-privacy-law-in-first-enforcement-action-under-a-state-comprehensive-data-privacy-law)
- **CFPB:** On **May 15, 2025** the CFPB formally withdrew its proposed rule "Protecting Americans from Harmful Data Broker Practices," which would have treated brokers as consumer reporting agencies under the FCRA. It said rulemaking was "not necessary or appropriate at this time" [S, 2025] — [Malwarebytes](https://www.malwarebytes.com/blog/news/2025/05/data-broker-protection-rule-quietly-withdrawn-by-cfpb)
- **DOJ Bulk Sensitive Data Rule (28 CFR Part 202, implementing EO 14117):** Effective April 8, 2025, with most provisions enforced from July 8, 2025. Security, audit and reporting duties for restricted transactions apply from Oct 6, 2025. The bulk threshold is **more than 100 US persons for human genomic data** and more than 1,000 for epigenomic, proteomic and transcriptomic data. The rule also covers biospecimens, and **de-identified, pseudonymized or encrypted data remains covered**. Civil penalties can reach twice the value of each violating transaction [S/P, 2025–2026] — [Reed Smith, Jul 2025](https://www.reedsmith.com/en/perspectives/2025/07/the-deadline-arrived-enforcement-doj-rule-bulk-transfers-sensitive-data); [Baker Donelson](https://www.bakerdonelson.com/doj-bulk-data-rule-key-takeaways-for-health-care-and-life-sciences); [Cornell decision tree, Feb 2026](https://researchservices.cornell.edu/sites/default/files/2026-02/Bulk%20Data%20Rule%20High-Level%20Decision%20Tree%202.26.26.pdf). A UCSF page cites a different date, Oct 6, 2026, for regulatory enforcement [S] — [UCSF](https://compliance.ucsf.edu/node/901). This conflict is unresolved.
- **Vermont:** It has a data broker registration law. Its current terms and any 2025 amendments were not verified this session.

### Inferences
- Because pseudonymized or encrypted data stays covered under the DOJ rule, "de-identify and sell" is not a safe harbor for genomic or health data going abroad. Buyers need transaction-level records showing who received what data.
- DROP turns deletion into an operational task brokers must handle at scale every 45 days. This lowers the long-term value of inventory gathered without consent.

### Gaps
- No MHMDA case outcomes or 2026 filings were found. Washington AG enforcement under the MHMDA was not found.
- The number of DROP sign-ups was not found.
- No DOJ enforcement actions under 28 CFR 202 were found.
- Details of any 2025–2026 amendments to the Connecticut, Nevada or Vermont laws were not found.

## 4. EU: GDPR fines involving health data or data sales, AI Act Article 10, Data Act

### Takeaway
GDPR fines total about €1.2B a year (2024 and 2025) and about €7.1B in total since 2018. Healthcare-specific fines are numerous but small in total (about €32M across 265 fines). Health-data cases focus on unlawful processing or sharing, such as Cegedim (€800k) and a Finnish online pharmacy (€1.1M). The AI Act's high-risk deadlines, including Article 10 data governance, were provisionally pushed back to Dec 2027 / Aug 2028.

### Cited Findings
- **GDPR totals:** About **€1.2B** in fines in 2025, roughly level with 2024, and **€7.1B** in total from May 25, 2018 to Jan 10, 2026. The Irish DPC has about €4.04B in total, and its largest 2025 fine was **€530M (Apr 2025)** for international transfers (TikTok, from background knowledge). Breach notifications rose 22% to 443 a day [P, Jan 2026] — [DLA Piper GDPR Fines Survey Jan 2026](https://www.dlapiper.com/en/insights/publications/2026/01/dla-piper-gdpr-fines-and-data-breach-survey-january-2026). A Freevacy summary gives €5.88B in total, which conflicts. It probably refers to the prior edition; rely on DLA Piper.
- **Healthcare fines:** DPAs in 27 countries have issued **265 healthcare fines totaling about €32.3M**, 44 more than the prior report. New healthcare fines in 2025 were **26% higher** than the previous period. The largest example cited is a **€1.1M** Finnish fine on an online pharmacy that let non-EU third-party web tools access customer data [S, May 2026] — [CMS Enforcement Tracker Report: Life Science & Healthcare](https://cms.law/en/int/publication/gdpr-enforcement-tracker-report/life-science-healthcare)
- **Cegedim Santé (France):** The CNIL fined it **€800,000** on Sept 5, 2024 (SAN-2024-013) for processing health data from about 2,000 doctors without the required authorization. The CNIL held the data was only pseudonymized and could be re-identified [S, 2024] — [Lefebvre Dalloz](https://open.lefebvre-dalloz.fr/actualites/droit-affaires/donnees-sante-cegedim-sante-ecope-amende-800-000-euros_f5f21113b-ce9b-4777-a2d3-31c654fec87a); [Next](https://next.ink/brief-article/800-000-euros-damende-pour-cegedim-qui-a-traite-des-donnees-de-sante-sans-autorisation/)
- **Meta €1.2B (May 2023):** The Irish DPC fined Meta over EU-US data transfers after an EDPB binding decision. It is the largest GDPR fine to date. It concerns transfers, not data sales (background knowledge, not verified this session) — [EDPB](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/news/news/2023/12-billion-euro-fine-facebook-result-edpb-binding-decision_en) (URL not fetched)
- **AI Act high-risk delay:** In Nov 2025 the Commission's Digital Omnibus proposed moving high-risk obligations from Aug 2026 to Dec 2027. A provisional political agreement (reported May 27, 2026) sets **Dec 2, 2027** for stand-alone Annex III systems and **Aug 2, 2028** for AI in Annex I regulated products. The changes take effect only once published in the Official Journal [S, 2026] — [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/); [OneTrust](https://www.onetrust.com/blog/eu-digital-omnibus-proposes-delay-of-ai-compliance-deadlines/). Whether it has been published as of Oct 2026 was not confirmed.
- **Article 10 contents (background knowledge, not verified this session):** Training, validation and test datasets for high-risk AI must follow data-governance practices covering collection processes and data origin, preparation, assumptions, bias examination and mitigation, and must be relevant, sufficiently representative and as free of errors as possible. Article 10(5) allows special-category data such as health data to be processed for bias detection only under strict safeguards — [AI Act Art. 10 text](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/10/) (URL not fetched)
- **Data Act:** It has applied since **Sept 12, 2025** (background knowledge, not verified this session). It is also covered by the Digital Omnibus [S] — [OneTrust](https://www.onetrust.com/blog/eu-digital-omnibus-proposes-delay-of-ai-compliance-deadlines/)

### Inferences
- Article 10's data-origin and data-governance documentation duty, even with the delay, means AI developers buying health training data will need documented provenance and lawful basis. This favors consent-based, auditable sources over broker-acquired datasets.
- EU enforcement on health data targets unlawful processing and pseudonymization that falls short, rather than outright "sales." The CNIL's Cegedim reasoning, that pseudonymized data is still personal data, matches the US DOJ rule's position.

### Gaps
- No GDPR fine specifically for *selling* health data was found. Whether the Omnibus has been formally adopted, and whether Article 10 itself changed, was not confirmed. Neither Article 10 nor the Data Act date was verified from primary text.

## 5. Korea: 민감정보 (sensitive data), PIPC 과징금 (penalty surcharges), 가명정보 (pseudonymized data), 전송요구권/마이데이터 (data portability / MyData)

### Takeaway
The Personal Information Protection Commission (PIPC, 개인정보위) has sharply increased penalty surcharges: SKT ₩134.8B (Aug 2025), KT ₩54B (Jul 2026), and Coupang ₩624.7B (Jun 2026, the largest ever, partly for unlawful collection of behavioral data). It has also sanctioned unauthorized sharing (KakaoPay ₩5.97B for transfers to Alipay) and sensitive-data profiling for ads (Meta ₩21.6B). At the same time, MyData portability was expanded from health and telecom to all sectors by an Enforcement Decree amendment in Feb 2026, with transfer duties starting Feb 20, 2027. This is a legal, consent-based channel for moving personal data.

### Cited Findings
- **Meta (Nov 2024):** **₩21.613B penalty surcharge plus ₩10.2M fine** for collecting sensitive data (religious and political views, same-sex marriage status) on about **980,000 Korean users** through Facebook profiles from Jul 2018 to Mar 2022, and providing it to about **4,000 advertisers** without a lawful basis such as separate consent [S, 2024] — [CIO Korea](https://www.cio.com/article/3599812/%ED%83%88%EB%B6%81%EC%9E%90-%EC%84%B1%EC%86%8C%EC%88%98%EC%9E%90-%EC%A2%85%EA%B5%90-%EC%A0%95%EB%B3%B4-%EA%B4%91%EA%B3%A0%EC%A3%BC%EC%97%90-%EB%84%98%EA%B2%A8%C2%B7%C2%B7%C2%B7.html); [SBS Biz](https://biz.sbs.co.kr/amp/article/20000200035)
- **Meta and Google (Sept 2022):** For collecting third-party behavioral data without consent for targeted ads: Meta ₩30.8B (confirmed in the search above [S] — [inews24](https://www.inews24.com/view/1780199)) and Google ₩69.2B (background knowledge, not verified this session).
- **KakaoPay (Jan 2025):** **₩5.968B** for providing personal data of about 40 million users to Alipay without consent, a breach of the overseas-transfer rules [S, 2025] — [Newspim](https://member.newspim.com/news/view/20250219000576); [Kukinews](https://www.kukinews.com/article/view/kuk202501230078)
- **SK Telecom (Aug 28, 2025):** **₩134.791B**, then the largest ever, for a USIM hack that leaked 25 data types for **23,244,649 users**. SKT filed an administrative lawsuit [S, 2025–2026] — [Digital Daily](https://www.ddaily.co.kr/page/view/2025082812323487903); [Boannews](https://m.boannews.com/html/detail.html?idx=141569)
- **Coupang (Jun 11, 2026):** **₩624.681B in total**, the largest ever: about ₩423.6B for a breach affecting **37.5M people** and **₩201.1B for collecting online activity records of more than 10M members without a legal basis**, plus a ₩16.8M fine. The legal cap is 3% of revenue, which on 2025 revenue of ₩45.5T would be about ₩1.36T. Coupang said it will contest the decision. Minutes released Sept 27, 2026 show the PIPC rejected Coupang's arguments for a lower penalty base [S, 2026] — [Herald K](https://www.heraldk.com/article/2026061018420873935); [Money Today](https://www.mt.co.kr/living/2026/06/11/2026061116372048921); [Metro Seoul, Sept 2026](https://www.metroseoul.co.kr/article/20260927500138). A first civil damages ruling (₩100,000 per person) was reported in July 2026 [S] — [Money Today](https://www.mt.co.kr/living/2026/07/31/2026073016142857922)
- **KT (Jul 2026):** **₩53.979B** for a femtocell-related leak [S, 2026] — [MTN](https://news.mtn.co.kr/news-detail/2026072911502939472); [BizWatch](https://news.bizwatch.co.kr/article/mobile/2026/07/30/0019)
- **MyData expansion (전송요구권):** On **Feb 10, 2026** the Cabinet approved an Enforcement Decree amendment expanding portability from health and telecom to **all sectors**, promulgated Feb 19, 2026. Covered entities are those with average revenue over ₩180B that process sensitive or unique-identifier data on 50,000+ people or personal data on 1M+ people, plus public-system operators. Transfer duties start **Feb 20, 2027** [S, 2026] — [Byline Network](https://byline.network/2026/03/11-633/); [Newsseoul](https://newsseoul.co.kr/news/view/1065598808261875). The PIPC and KISA ran a 2026 MyData service support program (applications Mar 11–Apr 13, 2026) [S] — [Newsseoul](https://newsseoul.co.kr/news/view/1065579620691546). A blog source reports a PIPA amendment promulgated Mar 10, 2026 and effective Sept 11, 2026 that strengthens data portability. ## 6. Trend: number of enforcement actions and total fines per year

### Takeaway
EU GDPR fines held steady at about €1.2B a year in 2024 and 2025, but healthcare fines are rising in number (+26% in 2025). Korea's PIPC penalties hit successive records in 2025–2026 (₩134.8B, then ₩624.7B). US federal money penalties remain small, while conduct bans and state and private enforcement grow.

### Cited Findings
- GDPR: about €1.2B in 2025, level with 2024; €7.1B in total since 2018; breach notifications up 22% to 443 a day [P, 2026] — [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en/insights/publications/2026/01/dla-piper-gdpr-fines-and-data-breach-survey-january-2026)
- EU healthcare: 265 fines (44 more than the prior report) totaling about €32.3M; 2025 healthcare fines 26% higher [S, 2026] — [CMS](https://cms.law/en/int/publication/gdpr-enforcement-tracker-report/life-science-healthcare)
- Korea record progression: SKT ₩134.8B (Aug 2025) → Coupang ₩624.7B (Jun 2026) [S] — [Herald K](https://www.heraldk.com/article/2026061018420873935)
- US FTC location/health orders by year: 2023 (GoodRx, BetterHelp) → 2024 (X-Mode, InMarket, Cerebral, HBNR rule) → Jan 2025 (Gravy, Mobilewalla) → 2026 (Kochava settlement, 13 PADFAA warning letters) [P/S] — see the sources in section 2.

### Inferences
- In all three jurisdictions, the direction is consistent: consent requirements are tightening and enforcement is shifting toward conduct bans plus provenance and audit duties, even where fine totals are flat (EU) or small (US FTC).

- No official annual count of FTC privacy actions for 2025 or 2026 was found. No annual PIPC total for penalty surcharges was found. These may be in the PIPC annual report (pipc.go.kr), which was not fetched.
