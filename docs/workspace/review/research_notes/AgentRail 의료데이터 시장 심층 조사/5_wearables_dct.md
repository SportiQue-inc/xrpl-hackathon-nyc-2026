# Wearables, Digital Biomarkers and Decentralized Clinical Trials as a Source of Person-Level Longitudinal Health Data (as of Oct 2026)

Method note: Direct page fetch was not used. Every figure below comes from WebSearch result summaries, so all are tagged **[secondary, via search summary]** unless the cited URL is the originating publisher/registry (tagged **[primary URL]**, but the content was still read through the search summary and not in full). ## 1. Scale of wearable / personal health data generation (market, ownership, rings, platform features, consumer CGM)

### Takeaway
About 611.5M wearables shipped worldwide in 2025 (IDC), and roughly half of US adults now own a wearable (Rock Health: 46% wearable, 57% any wearable or connected device, Dec 2025). Most of them are worn 5+ days a week. Growth is slowing: IDC sees about 2% unit growth in 2026, and Rock Health calls first-time adoption a plateau. Within that, the categories that produce medical-grade signals (smart rings, OTC CGM, FDA-cleared watch features) are growing fast. So the data is already being generated at population scale. The constraint is access to it, not collection.

### Cited Findings
**Shipments (IDC)**
- Global wearable shipments were **611.5M units in 2025, +9.1% YoY** [2025]. IDC credits launches, more price tiers, China subsidies and replacement demand. Earwear grew 7.8%. Apple and Huawei led smartwatch growth — [IDC Worldwide Wearable Device Market Results 2025](https://www.idc.com/promo/wearablevendor/vendor) [primary URL; read via search summary]
- IDC forecast: **+2.2% in 2026, +2.8% in 2027**. Memory-chip constraints are raising ASPs — [IDC](https://www.idc.com/promo/wearablevendor/vendor) [primary URL; via search summary]
- 2024 baseline: 534.6M units (+5.4%) [2024] — [IDC](https://www.idc.com/?p=4760) [primary URL; via search summary]. Note: 611.5M against 534.6M works out to about +14%, not +9.1%. IDC probably restated the 2024 base, so treat the two figures as coming from different tracker vintages.
- Unverified secondary claims (wristbands +14.7% in 2025, India −4%) come from a blog citing IDC. Not confirmed — [voxbooster blog](https://voxbooster.com/blog/wearables-statistics-2026) [secondary, low quality]

### Inferences
- If ~46% of 260M US adults own a wearable, that is roughly 120M people, and ~83% of them wear it 5+ days/week. Large research cohorts hold tens of thousands of wearable participants (All of Us ~60k, UK Biobank ~100k, see Q4), so research captures well under 0.1% of the US wearable population. This is an inference from the cited numbers, not a sourced statistic.
- Oura (~5M paying members, ~$1.6B+ annualized revenue) and OTC CGM show that consumers pay for continuous physiological monitoring. Those data streams stay in vendor clouds, which is the gap a person-side agent could bridge.
- Apple's hypertension model was trained on more than 100k participants. That shows platform owners can assemble research-scale cohorts, but they do it inside proprietary programs.

## 2. Digital biomarkers and DCT market size; share of trials using DHTs/wearables; FDA/EMA regulatory acceptance

### Takeaway
Market sizes are soft vendor estimates: digital biomarkers about $4–6B (2025) and DCT about $9B (2025). Wearables still appear in only about 2% of registered trials, and in under 1% as endpoints. The regulatory path does exist: the FDA DHT final guidance (Dec 2023), the first EMA-qualified wearable primary endpoint (SV95C, 2023), and the first FDA MDDT qualification of a consumer wearable feature (Apple AFib History, 2024).

### Cited Findings
**Market sizes (vendor estimates; wide disagreement)**
- Digital biomarkers: **$6.29B (2025) → $17.73B (2031), 19.1% CAGR** — [MarketsandMarkets](https://www.marketsandmarkets.com/Market-Reports/digital-biomarker-market-7725399.html) [secondary/vendor]
- Digital biomarkers: **$4.31B (2025) → $30.79B (2035), 21.7% CAGR** — [Spherical Insights](https://www.sphericalinsights.com/blogs/top-20-companies-in-global-digital-biomarker-market-industry-intelligence-report-by-spherical-insights-2026-2035) [secondary/vendor]
- Digital biomarkers: $3.9B (2023), 28.8% CAGR 2025–2032 — [SkyQuest via GII](https://www.giikorea.co.kr/report/sky1701040-digital-biomarkers-market-size-share-growth.html) [secondary/vendor]
- Decentralized and digitalized clinical trials: **$8.71B (2025) → $17.92B (2035), 7.5% CAGR** — [Towards Healthcare](https://www.towardshealthcare.com/insights/decentralized-and-digitalized-clinical-trials-market-sizing) [secondary/vendor]
- DCT: **$9.2B (2025) → $34.6B (2034), 14.8% CAGR** — [Market Intelo](https://marketintelo.com/report/decentralized-clinical-trials-market/amp) [secondary/vendor, low quality]

**Share of trials using DHTs/wearables**
- As of Dec 2023, **~1,550 trials worldwide used wearables, ~2% of all trials** — [Medable](https://www.medable.com/tags/wearables) [secondary/vendor]
- Wearables appear in **~2.2% of trials in high-income countries since 2020**, and **<1% of ClinicalTrials.gov trials report wearables as primary or secondary endpoints** — [Applied Clinical Trials, Nov 2023](https://www.appliedclinicaltrialsonline.com/view/why-wearable-dhts-are-bringing-more-meaningful-data-collection-to-patient-centric-clinical-trials) [secondary]
- Marra et al. 2020 (npj Digit Med): an 18-year ClinicalTrials.gov analysis found **~34% CAGR** in trials using connected digital products, with **more than 1,100 unique trials using one in 2017 and in 2018** — [PMC7125096](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7125096/) [primary, peer-reviewed; via search summary]
- Masanneck et al. 2023 (npj Digit Med): DHT use in ClinicalTrials.gov trials for epilepsy, MS, Alzheimer's and Parkinson's grew for both established measures (motor) and novel ones (speech) — [PMC9918454](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9918454/) [primary, peer-reviewed; via search summary]
- An unverified blog claims DHT use rose from 0.7% of trials (2010) to 11.4% (2020). It cites a PMC article that was not checked — [x-cube labs feed](https://cms.xcubelabs.com/tag/wearable-healthcare/feed/) [secondary, unverified]
- An old forecast (Kaiser Associates/Intel, cited 2021) said "70% of trials will use wearables by 2025". Actual uptake (~2%) was far lower — [Worldwide Clinical Trials whitepaper](https://www.worldwide.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/07/Corporate-Wearables-Quality-Whitepaper-20212307-1.pdf) [secondary]

**FDA DHT guidance**
- FDA final guidance "**Digital Health Technologies for Remote Data Acquisition in Clinical Investigations**", **Dec 22, 2023**. It covers drugs, biologics and devices and ties to FDORA §3607(a). Changes from the draft address DHTs that meet the device definition, DHT selection and rationale, data retention and protection, and DHT updates during trials. If the sponsor shows a DHT is "fit-for-purpose" through verification and validation, FDA does not intend to assess design-control compliance for uncleared DHTs used only for trial data capture. An IDE may still be required in some cases — [FDA Law Blog (Hyman Phelps)](https://www.thefdalawblog.com/2024/02/fda-releases-final-guidance-on-use-of-digital-health-technologies-for-remote-data-acquisition-in-clinical-investigations/); [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/healthcare/1425664/fda-releases-final-guidance-on-use-of-digital-health-technologies-for-remote-data-acquisition-in-clinical-investigations); [CM Health Law](https://www.cmhealthlaw.com/2023/12/fda-releases-guidance-on-digital-health-technologies-for-clinical-investigations/) [secondary, law-firm summaries]

**Qualified digital endpoints / tools**
- **SV95C (stride velocity 95th centile, ankle-worn sensor, Duchenne MD)**: EMA qualified it as a **secondary endpoint in 2019** (age ≥5), the first wearable-derived digital endpoint qualified by EMA. In **July 2023 EMA qualified it as a primary endpoint** (age ≥4, superiority studies, alternative to 6MWT), the first digital primary endpoint qualified. The evidence package covered 125 ambulant DMD patients from 6 studies. FDA review in the COA Qualification Program was reported as pending, and no FDA decision was found — [BioSpace / SYSNAV release](https://www.biospace.com/sysnav-healthcare-announces-ema-primary-endpoint-qualification-of-stride-velocity-95th-centile-sv95c-for-duchenne-muscular-dystrophy) [secondary, company release]; [Servais et al., J Neuromuscul Dis 2021, PMC8460979](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8460979) [primary, peer-reviewed; via summary]; [PMC11606965](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11606965) [primary]
- **Apple Watch AFib History: the first DHT qualified under FDA's MDDT program (May 2024)**. It is qualified as a biomarker test estimating AFib burden as a **secondary effectiveness endpoint in cardiac ablation device studies**, supported by 2 cohorts totaling 280 subjects. Sponsors can reference the MDDT in submissions. Note that qualification is not consumer clearance — [FDAnews](https://fdanews.com/articles/print/213627-afib-history-apple-watch-feature-qualified-for-use-in-medical-device-development); [IQVIA blog](https://iqvia.com/blogs/2024/05/apple-watch-afib-history-feature-makes-medical-device-history) [secondary]

### Inferences
- Regulators will accept wearable data as trial evidence when it is fit-for-purpose and validated. The bottleneck is not regulatory permission. It is data quality, continuity and provenance, which an agent that time-aligns data with clear provenance could address.
- The ~2% trial penetration, against ~50% consumer ownership, is a strong "untapped supply" contrast for the pitch.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed 2024–2026 ClinicalTrials.gov analysis giving the current % of trials using DHTs was found.
- The FDA "DHT framework" document (FDA's 2023 framework for DHTs in drug development under PDUFA VII) and the DHT Steering Committee were not verified in this pass.
- FDA status of SV95C as of Oct 2026 is unknown.
- CTTI DHT recommendations and DiMe's library of digital endpoints were not retrieved.

## 3. Retention, missing data, wear-time compliance, incentives

### Takeaway
Remote digital studies lose most participants within days. Pratap 2020 found a median retention of 5.5 days across 100k+ participants. Even the landmark Apple Heart Study saw heavy attrition after notification. Retention improves with clinician referral (+~40 days) and compensation (+~22 days). Device-based protocols with posted devices (UK Biobank) achieve high wear compliance but only ~45% response. Financial incentives raise activity but do not remove missing data (12–24% missing step-days in an incentive arm).

### Cited Findings
- **Pratap et al. 2020, npj Digital Medicine** (8 app-based remote studies, 2014–2019, more than 100,000 participants): median retention per study ranged **2–26 days, overall median 5.5 days**. **Clinician referral** was associated with about **+40 days** median retention, **compensation** about **+22 days** (one study only), having the condition of interest about +7 days, and older age about +4 days. Samples were also unrepresentative — [PMC7026051](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7026051/) [primary, peer-reviewed; via search summary]; [MobiHealthNews](https://www.mobihealthnews.com/news/low-retention-unrepresentative-samples-prevalent-among-large-app-based-digital-health-studies) [secondary]
- **Apple Heart Study** (Perez et al., NEJM 2019): **419,297 participants** enrolled over 8 months, with median monitoring of 117 days. **2,161 (0.52%) received irregular-pulse notifications**, ranging from 3.2% at age ≥65 to 0.16% at age <40. Only **450 returned analyzable ECG patches**, about 21% of those notified. AF was found in 34% of returned patches, and PPV was 0.84. Healthcare Dive reports that **1,216 of the notified never initiated the first telemedicine visit** — [Stanford Medicine news](https://med.stanford.edu/news/all-news/2019/11/through-apple-heart-study--stanford-medicine-researchers-show-we.html) [primary institution; via summary]; [ACC Journal Scan](https://www.acc.org/Latest-in-Cardiology/Journal-Scans/2019/11/14/15/26/Large-Scale-Assessment-of-a-Smartwatch) [secondary]; [Healthcare Dive](https://www.healthcaredive.com/news/landmark-apple-watch-afib-study-yields-mixed-results/567347/) [secondary]
- **UK Biobank accelerometer** (Doherty et al. 2017, PLOS ONE): **103,712 datasets, 44.8% response rate**. Median wear time was **6.9 of 7 days** (IQR 6.5–7.0), and **93.3% (96,600) had valid data** — [UK Biobank publication page](https://www.ukbiobank.ac.uk/publications/large-scale-population-assessment-of-physical-activity-using-wrist-worn-accelerometers-the-uk-biobank-study/); [PLOS ONE PDF via UKB](https://biobank.ctsu.ox.ac.uk/ukb/ukb/docs/acc_plos_0169649.pdf) [primary; via summary]
- **All of Us Fitbit wear-time**: a preprint analyzing 11,901 All of Us participants found wear time varies by demographics, social determinants, lifestyle, mental health and disease. It proposes compliance thresholds, wear-time covariate adjustment and adaptive thresholds to retain data — [JMIR preprint 46149](https://preprints.jmir.org/preprint/46149) [primary preprint, not peer-reviewed; via summary]. All of Us provides Fitbit data "as generated by Fitbit webAPI" with **no cleaning**, so wear-time filtering is left to the researcher — [All of Us support](https://support.researchallofus.org/hc/en-us/articles/20281023493908) [primary]
- Baroudi et al., JMIR mHealth 2025 (3 Fitbit datasets): populations with **daily wear time under ~15 h/day** were the most sensitive to analytic choices — [JMIR mHealth 2025 e46149](https://mhealth.jmir.org/2025/1/e46149/PDF) [primary; via summary]
- **Incentives**: ACTIVE REWARD (ischemic heart disease, n=105) paid $14/week with $2 deducted per missed goal day. Even in the incentive arm, **11.6% and 23.9% of step-count days were missing** across 2 phases, and step-count differences were significant only in later phases in adjusted models — [PMC6220542](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6220542) [primary; via summary]. In a 1,062-participant, one-year trial in high cardiovascular risk patients, the loss-framed $14/week incentive group raised steps the most and sustained the gain about 6 months post-study — [Business Insurance](https://www.businessinsurance.com/article/20240416/NEWS06/912363877/Workers-with-financial-incentive-more-likely-to-step-up) [secondary]
- Systematic reviews: Beauchamp et al. 2020 (25 oncology wearable studies, only 3 RCTs) concluded that adherence, and how it is defined, is crucial and inconsistently reported — [JMIR mHealth 2020 e22006](https://mhealth.jmir.org/2020/11/e22006). A JMIR 2022 review (studies 2014–2019) found inconsistent adherence definitions for biometric monitoring technologies and proposed minimum reporting standards — [Newswise summary](https://www.newswise.com/articles/recommendations-for-defining-and-reporting-adherence-measured-by-biometric-monitoring-technologies-systematic-review) [secondary]

### Inferences
- The retention problem in remote studies is structural. Recruiting new participants into a new app, starting from zero, means losing them in days. By contrast, consumers already wear devices 5+ days/week (Rock Health 83%). An agent that taps data people already generate passively, including retroactive history, sidesteps the "day-1 app install" attrition curve.
- Clinician referral (+40 days) and compensation (+22 days) are the two strongest retention levers. That supports a design that links clinic data and pays contributors.
- Unharmonized raw data (All of Us Fitbit "no cleaning") and variable wear time push missing-data handling onto every researcher. A standardized time axis with wear-time and provenance metadata is a concrete value-add.

### Gaps
- No pooled cross-trial missing-data rate for wearable endpoints was found.
- Apple Heart Study follow-up/attrition numbers differ across secondary sources (exclusions of 1,711 vs other framings). Check the NEJM paper (doi:10.1056/NEJMoa1901183) before quoting.
- No 2024–2026 retention metrics for large BYOD programs (e.g., All of Us active sync rates) were found.

## 4. Large wearable research programs (participants, data volume)

### Takeaway
The largest research-grade wearable datasets have tens of thousands to about 100k participants. All of Us has about 60k Fitbit participants with linked EHR and genomics. UK Biobank has about 100k with a single 7-day accelerometer week. Apple has run 400k+ enrollments through app-based studies. Linkage of wearable data to EHR, genomics and surveys is the scarce, high-value property: only 46% of All of Us Fitbit participants have the full set.

### Cited Findings
- **All of Us (NIH)**: the March 2025 data release contains **nearly 60,000 participants with Fitbit data**, 4x the prior release and described as the world's largest publicly available Fitbit dataset. The release covers more than 633,000 participants overall — [UW CSDE news](https://csde.washington.edu/news-events/new-nih-all-of-us-research-program-expands-data-offering) [secondary, relaying NIH]. RTI describes the dataset as more than 59,000 participants spanning 14 years. **46% of Fitbit participants also have EHR, physical measurements, genomics and survey data** — [RTI](https://www.rti.org/publication/the-all-of-us-research-programs-wearables-dataset) [primary-ish publication page; via summary]. There are two routes in: BYOD consent via the Participant Portal, and the WEAR Study, where Fitbits are given to eligible participants (Scripps-led, launched 2021; an original ~10,000-device distribution) — [All of Us support](https://support.researchallofus.org/hc/en-us/articles/20281023493908); [Scripps Research](https://www.scripps.edu/news-events/news/20210224-aou-fitbit-study/) [primary]
- **UK Biobank**: 103,712 participants with 7-day wrist Axivity AX3 recordings at 100 Hz triaxial (2013–2015), 96,600 of them valid — [UK Biobank](https://www.ukbiobank.ac.uk/publications/large-scale-population-assessment-of-physical-activity-using-wrist-worn-accelerometers-the-uk-biobank-study/) [primary]
- **Apple Heart Study (Stanford/Apple)**: 419,297 participants (2017–2018) — see Q3 — [Stanford Medicine](https://med.stanford.edu/news/all-news/2019/11/through-apple-heart-study--stanford-medicine-researchers-show-we.html)
- **Apple's hypertension feature** used training data from multiple studies totaling **more than 100,000 participants** and a validation study with more than 2,000 [2025] — [Engadget](https://engadget.com/wearables/apple-watch-series-11-receives-fda-clearance-for-hypertension-alerts-120046138.html) [secondary]
- **Verily Project Baseline (with Stanford and Duke)**: launched 2017 with a target of **~10,000 participants followed ≥4 years**. Participants used the daily Study Watch, a sleep sensor and a home hub, with annual site visits for blood, genomics, imaging and ECG, stored on Google Cloud. Raw Study Watch data was not returned to participants — [MobiHealthNews](https://www.mobihealthnews.com/content/verily-stanford-and-duke-kick-project-baseline-study-develop-broad-reference-human-health); [Xconomy](https://legacy.xconomy.com/?p=343782) [secondary, 2017]

### Inferences
- Even the flagship public wearable dataset (All of Us, ~60k) is tiny against ~120M US wearable owners (inference). Its linked subset (46%) is what makes it valuable, which directly supports the "one time axis across wearable + clinic + survey" pitch.
- Programs that hand out devices (UK Biobank, WEAR, Baseline) buy compliance but give short windows (7 days) or small n (10k). BYOD gives scale and long history (14 years) but unharmonized data and variable wear.

### Gaps
- **Apple Heart and Movement Study** (Brigham/AHA, launched 2019) and the Apple Women's Health and Hearing studies: current enrollment was not verified.
- Project Baseline: current enrollment and data volume were not found. Only 2017 launch plans were available.
- No data-volume figures (TB, person-days) for All of Us Fitbit or UK Biobank were found.

## 5. Remote patient monitoring (RPM) reimbursement as an indicator of clinical use

### Takeaway
Medicare RPM use grew about 10x, from ~55k enrollees (2019) to ~570k (2022), with ~$300M in 2022 payments (OIG 2024). OIG flagged weak oversight: 43% of enrollees did not receive all three billing components. CMS's CY2026 PFS added short-duration codes (99445 for 2–15 days of data, 99470 for the first 10 minutes of management), lowering the bar for billing intermittent device data. That makes device data a more routinely captured part of the clinical record.

### Cited Findings
- **HHS OIG report (Sep 24, 2024)**: Medicare RPM enrollees rose from **~55,000 (2019) to ~570,000 (2022)**. Payments were **~$300M+ in 2022 ($311M in one summary) vs $15M in 2019**. Average per-enrollee cost was $545 (2022) vs $266 (2019). **43% of enrollees did not receive all three components** (education/setup, device supply, treatment management). Medicare lacks data on who ordered RPM and what was measured. OIG warned of unsolicited-enrollment fraud — [AHA News](https://www.aha.org/news/headline/2024-09-27-oig-more-oversight-needed-remote-patient-monitoring-medicare); [Manatt](https://manatt.com/insights/newsletters/health-highlights/oig-report-calls-for-additional-oversight-of-remot); [TechTarget](https://techtarget.com/virtualhealthcare/news/366612003/Remote-patient-monitoring-use-raises-HHS-OIG-concerns) [secondary]
