Not legal advice; designed to the requirements, not “compliant”. Mongolia A/90 server rule reviewed before launch. Demo: synthetic data, Testnet.

Using personal health data comes with legal conditions on consent, withdrawal, cross-border transfer and payment. We built those into code. In all three countries the first basis is the person's own explicit consent per use. Separate consents, re-checked every round and before the key is released. In Korea even viewing from abroad counts as a transfer, so we ask once for cross-border consent per institution and country, and we push data rather than give read access. In the US, a personal agent the person picks is outside HIPAA, but likely a PHR vendor under the FTC rule, so one out-of-scope share would be a notifiable breach; scope checks are a legal control, not UX. Mongolia: PDPL 14.1 consent allows transfer; the A/90 server-location rule gets a written opinion before launch, and raw Mongolian genomes are out of the MVP. We say "designed to the requirements", never "compliant".
