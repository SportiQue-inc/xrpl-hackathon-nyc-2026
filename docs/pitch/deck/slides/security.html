The security layer underneath

Money, keys and consent are tied together on the ledger.

Payment is the key

The data key K is the escrow’s preimage. The transaction that pays is the one that unlocks.

Stamped at intake

Hospitals and devices sign records as they enter her DB. A tampered week fails before payment: 0 RLUSD.

Push, not read

Sealed end to end to the buyer’s key. Nobody gets read access to her store; the platform holds no keys or funds.

One order, one record

Consent hash, bundle hash and escrow are bound into one trade record anyone can check.

Withdrawal on-chain

Her research licence (MPT) is clawed back; unreleased rounds are refunded. Delivered rounds can’t be recalled, and she is told first.

An agent that says no

Expired ethics credential, out-of-scope field, “ignore consent” hidden in a record: refused, 0 orders.

Underneath sits the security layer. The key that opens the data is the escrow's preimage, so paying and unlocking are one transaction. Provenance is stamped when records enter the person's database, so a tampered week is blocked before any payment. Data is pushed sealed to the buyer; nobody gets read access, and the platform never holds keys or funds. If she withdraws, the research licence is clawed back on the ledger and unreleased rounds are refunded. And her agent turns money down: expired ethics approval, out-of-scope fields, or a prompt injection hidden in a medical record. 25 attack replays are in the test suite.
